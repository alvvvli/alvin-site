# Rotors at ILA 2026: an attack-helicopter debut and a fleet in transition
Fixed-wing gets the headline flypasts, but the rotary side of ILA 2026 was arguably where the more consequential story played out — a clean-sheet European attack helicopter looking for a home, right as Germany's current one heads toward retirement.

## Field notes, rotor by rotor
- **AW249's big moment** — Leonardo's AW249 (military designation AH-249A, nicknamed "Fenice") made its first appearance in an airshow flying-display programme here, after two prior static-only outings. It's billed as the only clean-sheet Western combat helicopter in development, built around a tandem-seat cockpit with a large-area touchscreen display and a Leonardo-developed Battle Management System.
- **The gap it's chasing** — Germany's Heeresflieger is retiring its Tiger attack helicopter by 2031, with an armed H145M as an interim stopgap. Leonardo is pitching the AW249, with German industrial participation, as the long-term replacement — a decision that isn't made yet.
- **Airbus's rotor lineup** — H225 and NH90 covering security missions and troop mobility, H145M getting incremental capability upgrades, and PioneerLab — a modified twin-engine H145 technology demonstrator — on static display testing a rotor-strike alerting system as its first "techno-brick," with automated take-off and landing next on the list.
- **The speed demonstrator** — Airbus's Racer high-speed helicopter, targeting a cruise speed above 400 km/h and roughly 25% lower fuel burn than a same-class conventional helicopter, flew in the display alongside the Tiger and the widebody A350.
- **Where Germany builds its helicopters** — Donauwörth is Airbus's main German helicopter site, assembling the H135, H145, H145M and NH90 — worth knowing next time one of these types is spotted anywhere in Europe.
