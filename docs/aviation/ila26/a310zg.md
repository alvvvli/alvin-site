# Airbus A310 ZERO-G: 22 seconds of weightlessness, engineered on repeat
Tucked into the Space Pavilion crowd was an aircraft with a stranger job description than anything else on the apron: falling out of the sky on purpose, over and over, so scientists get a brief window without gravity.

## The physics of a "popular classic" on the static park
- **The manoeuvre** — the aircraft pulls up to roughly a 47–50° climb angle, holding passengers at around 1.8g, before the thrust is cut at the top of the arc. What follows is a parabolic freefall giving about 22 seconds of near-weightlessness before the next pull-out.
- **Rinse and repeat** — a typical campaign day flies 31 parabolas back to back, flown out of Bordeaux-Mérignac and, for the ILA-linked sets, over German airspace.
- **A German jet with a past life** — this particular A310 rolled off the line in 1989, flew for the former East German carrier Interflug, and later served as a German government transport nicknamed "Konrad Adenauer." Lufthansa Technik converted it for parabolic flight, and operator Novespace (a CNES subsidiary based in Bordeaux) qualified it with EASA and the French civil aviation authority before it entered service in 2015, taking over from the retired A300 ZERO-G.
- **Who it flies for** — CNES, ESA and the German Space Agency at DLR are the main users, running experiments across biology, medicine, physics, materials science and technology testing, plus astronaut training ahead of ISS missions.
- **Space Pavilion context** — this year's pavilion opening drew Germany's federal research minister Dorothee Bär alongside ESA Director General Josef Aschbacher and ESA astronauts Alexander Gerst and Matthias Maurer — a reminder of how much of "space" work still happens inside an airliner cabin over the Atlantic or the German countryside.

Standing next to it on the tarmac, it just looks like an old A310 in a French livery. It's only once you picture 31 dives a day, at 1.8g on the way up and near-zero on the way over the top, that the airframe fatigue budget on this jet starts to feel genuinely impressive.
