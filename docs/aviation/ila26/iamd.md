# IAMD at ILA 2026: Europe's air-defence shopping list, laid out on the tarmac
If one theme quietly overtook the flying display this year, it was ground-based air and missile defence. The Defence Park was full of vehicles and radars rather than airframes, and the conversations happening there may end up mattering more than any flypast.

## What's actually being layered together
- **Skyranger 30** — Rheinmetall's mobile short-range air defence system, mounted on a Boxer 8x8 wheeled vehicle and paired with MBDA's DefendAir guided missile. The core weapon is a 30x173mm revolver cannon with a roughly 3,000-metre effective range, using programmable airburst ("Ahead") ammunition tuned for a high hit-probability against small drones; the turret is modular enough to add short-range missiles for longer reach. Several EU and NATO members, Germany included, have already adopted it.
- **Arrow 3** — Israel Aerospace Industries brought its exo-atmospheric anti-ballistic missile interceptor, the system Germany acquired in 2023 and received delivery of the following year. A second order, discussed around the show, was framed as a marker of how far German-Israeli defence cooperation has moved. IAI's BARAK MX multi-layered air defence system, deployable on land, at sea or mobile, was on display alongside it.
- **A hybrid counter-drone concept** — MBDA Germany unveiled a combined DefendAir–DEWS-L system: 24 short-range interceptor missiles and a high-energy laser weapon sharing a single palletised turret, built on a joint venture with Rheinmetall (announced back in January 2026) aimed at fielding operational laser weapons.
- **The paperwork that matters most** — Airbus Defence and Space signed a new agreement with Diehl Defence specifically to deepen Integrated Air and Missile Defence cooperation, one of several agreements at the show pointing toward AI-enabled sensing, autonomous decision support and counter-UAS integration across the continent's air-defence supply chain.
- **The driver, stated plainly by more than one exhibitor** — ground-based air defence has become a much bigger European priority since the war in Ukraine, and mass drone and loitering-munition use is the specific capability gap everyone at the Defence Park was trying to close.
