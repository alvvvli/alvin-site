# Eurofighter EF2000: a 1990s airframe getting a 2050s avionics fit
The Typhoon was one of the loudest, most repeated acts in the ILA 2026 flying display, and for good reason — Germany used the show to make clear this jet isn't a bridge to something else, it's the backbone plan through the 2050s.

## What's actually changing under the skin
- **Tranche 4, rolled out days before the show** — Airbus unveiled Germany's latest Tranche 4 Typhoon at its Manching plant on 20 May 2026, headlined by the Hensoldt ECRS Mk1 AESA radar (Germany's own variant, distinct from the UK's ECRS Mk2) and a single 12-by-22-inch Large Area Display replacing the old trio of 6-by-6-inch screens.
- **A new "EK" sub-fleet** — outside the Tranche programme, Germany is converting 15 existing Eurofighters into a dedicated electronic-warfare variant fitted with Saab's Arexis EW suite and AGM-88E anti-radiation missiles, taking over the suppression-of-enemy-air-defences job from the retiring Tornado ECR fleet.
- **New stores on the pylons at ILA** — Rafael's Litening 5 targeting pod (the Luftwaffe has ordered 90 to replace older Litening III units; the pod can identify and track targets from over 60 miles out), Spice 250 precision-guided munitions, and the KEPD 350 Taurus cruise missile were all shown integrated on Typhoon this year.
- **A wingman in the works** — Airbus is pairing the Eurofighter with the U740 Valkyrie, its designation for a Kratos XQ-58A-based uncrewed collaborative combat aircraft intended to fly deep-strike and ISR profiles ahead of a crewed Typhoon.
- **Not just a German story** — the consortium behind the jet (Airbus, BAE Systems, Leonardo) now has over 600 Typhoons flying across nine operators, and Spain's air force also brought its own Eurofighters to Berlin for static and flying display.
