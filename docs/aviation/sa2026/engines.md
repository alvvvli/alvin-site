# Engines at Singapore Airshow 2026: robots on the shop floor, orders in the air
The engine story at this show split cleanly into two threads — how fast MRO shops can turn engines around, and how confident airlines are in ordering more of them despite a rough couple of years for one engine family in particular.

## What stood out
- **A robot named Alfred** — Pratt & Whitney's Eagle Services Asia centre in Singapore showed off its automation for servicing PW1100 and GTF engines. The robot works inside a roughly 26-by-20-foot "pen," loading rotors into an industrial oven, waiting for them to cool, then transferring them into a hydraulic stacking rig. The task that used to take around 14 hours of manual work now takes about half that, with roughly an 87.5% cut in total manhours.
- **Vietjet's vote of confidence** — Vietjet Air chose Pratt & Whitney GTF engines for an additional 44 A320neo-family aircraft, including 20 A321XLRs, pushing its GTF-powered fleet to 137 aircraft. Notable given that around a third of the global GTF fleet was grounded for technical issues at the time — this was read as a strong signal in P&W's improved production process.
- **APU coverage, not just engines** — Scoot signed a 15-year maintenance agreement with Pratt & Whitney Canada covering 24 APS5000 auxiliary power units across its Boeing 787 Dreamliner fleet.
- **The usual suspects, present in force** — GE Aerospace and Rolls-Royce both exhibited, alongside RTX's engine businesses, reflecting how much of the show's floor space at Changi is now given over to propulsion and its aftermarket rather than airframes.
