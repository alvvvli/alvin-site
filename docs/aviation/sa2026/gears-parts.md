# Landing gear & parts: the least glamorous, most decisive deal of the show
No flying display, no ribbon cutting — just a contract announcement about landing gear. And yet it was, by Boeing's own description, the biggest deal of its kind the company has ever signed.

## The parts story, in numbers
- **Record-setting exchange deal** — Boeing announced its largest-ever Landing Gear Exchange agreement, covering more than 75 aircraft across the Singapore Airlines Group's 737 MAX and 787 fleets. The programme gives the airline group scheduling flexibility on gear overhauls, aiming to optimise service life while cutting aircraft-on-ground time.
- **Why it matters more than it sounds** — with global spares shortages still a live problem across the region, exchange programmes like this let carriers swap in a serviceable gear assembly from Boeing's managed inventory rather than waiting out a full overhaul cycle on their own unit.
- **A parts play that touches multiple fleet types** — the deal spans both the SIA Group's narrowbody 737 MAX aircraft and its 787 widebodies, plus its low-cost subsidiary Scoot, underlining how a single supply-chain agreement can ripple across an entire group's fleet mix.
- **The bigger backdrop** — regional show coverage kept circling back to the same point: appetite for single-aisle and regional aircraft is intensifying across Vietnam, Cambodia and Malaysia, and every one of those aircraft eventually needs the same landing gear, spares and distribution network this deal is built on.
