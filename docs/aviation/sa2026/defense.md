# Defense at Singapore Airshow 2026: the region's rearmament, on full display
The 10th edition leaned harder into defense than any recent show, and the static park and aerobatic schedule both read like a snapshot of where Asia-Pacific military spending is actually headed in 2026.

## Notes from the flight line and the static park
- **A rare PLA appearance** — China's August 1st ("Ba Yi") aerobatic team flew eight Chengdu J-10C fighters in a rare overseas display, reportedly refuelling in the air for the whole transit; a large-scale model of the J-35 stealth fighter sat in space China rented behind ST Engineering's main booth.
- **RSAF's drone pivot** — the Republic of Singapore Air Force was handed a large share of the static park, showing its F-15SG and F-16C fast jets alongside its shift toward Orbiter 4 and Hermes 900 uncrewed aircraft — organisers framed this as evidence that a "drone-centric" force posture is now the operational baseline, not a future concept.
- **Regional display teams returned** — India's Sarang team flew its five HAL Dhruv helicopters (one of only three times it's performed outside India, all in Singapore), and Indonesia's Jupiter Aerobatic Team brought its six KAI KT-1B Wongbee trainers, trained in part by the RAAF's Roulettes.
- **A canceled deal made headlines too** — Boeing confirmed in February it was no longer pursuing the F-15ID variant for Indonesia, ending a 2023 memorandum of understanding for 24 aircraft — a reminder that not every show story is a new contract.
- **New hardware on the ground** — Rafael showed off new loitering-munition variants, the L-SPIKE 1X and 4X; Ondas Holdings launched a unified counter-drone and ground-defense portfolio; and ST Engineering unveiled its own EagleStrike loitering munition.
- **Space entered the conversation** — the first Space Summit ran alongside the airshow, and Singapore announced it would establish a National Space Agency, which was formally stood up in April.
