The Stuttgart S-DT 8.16 provided a useful comparison with the larger regional and intercity vehicles exhibited nearby.

Walking through the passenger compartment, I examined its seating arrangements, interior circulation, articulated sections, and passenger information displays. The interior reflects a different set of priorities from long-distance rolling stock, with greater emphasis on frequent boarding, standing passengers, and efficient use of space.

It was a useful reminder that railway vehicle engineering is highly dependent on operating context. A light-rail vehicle serving an urban network requires a very different balance of capacity, comfort, accessibility, and operational efficiency from a high-speed or regional train.
